Last night's rain was long gone this morning, and although it was cloudy, it wasn't nearly as foggy as expected. Breakfast was the usual oatmeal, banana, and half of a blueberry muffin eaten at our new table on the porch, while we watched the clouds and felt the wind blowing them away. 

When we emerged a little while later from our room, ready to set out on our little adventure, the sun was already blindingly bright! So nice. It was still very windy, though, and the wind was about to get even worse.

The adventure I had suggested for today came about after I noticed the entry for Cranberry Hill in Matthew Marchon's book (_The Acadia You Haven't Seen_, volume 2). Rich had just mentioned yesterday that he liked the Schooner Head Path, and I thought that making Cranberry Hill our goal with Schooner Head Trail the means to reach it would provide a nice low-key adventure for our morning.

Based on the description in the book, it seemed that the better option would be to park at the south end, at the Schooner Head parking area, and walk north from there. We expected to find Cranberry Hill at about .6 miles from the four-way intersection at Schooner Head. While heading over toward the road, we came across the little "shortcut" that cuts a diagonal from the parking area to the point where the Schooner Head Trail crosses the road - I think this is actually the start of the trail now and not jsut an add-on. I had seen it on the map, but Rich had never recalled noticing it before. 

This was a peaceful trail, one that Rich found particularly pretty. He was poking around taking some photos of mushrooms when I noticed what looked like an old road grade going off into the distance past a large rock and a concrete cube - things that the park service often uses to either obscure old roads or paths or just keep vehicles from driving on them. We decided to follow the path, which in places certainly looked like it had been a wide path or even a road at one time, and it kept going, and gonig, all the way down to the shore! It emerged at a rocky cove not far from Anemone Cave.

While I poked around and made sure to get a tracklog of the old path, Rich took som emore photos of mushrooms we found along the way, and the view over the bay from the end of the path. (And he took a little tumble, too, when a branch he was leaning on to try to get a photo of me out on the rocks gave way, but no damage was done.) 

This was an exciting find, and we hadn't even come near to our goal for the morning! So we hiked back out to the Schooner Head Path and continued on for another few tenths of a mile to look for the spot where we should enter the woods to climb Cranberry Hill.

The directions in the book were accurate enough that we knew right away when we were in the correct spot. Just knowing that we needed to start right after the boundary marker for the park was enough. We coudl basically see the summit from teh road, and we headed straight toward it through mostly open woods, only switchbacking here and there to get around the steepest chunkiest rocks.

Marchon was right, this certainly offers a payoff for such a simple hike! There are views of Champlain, where we could actually watch people as they climbed up the Precipice Trail - no technical climbers on the main slabs, though, and there is even a partially-obstructed view over the bay which Marchon didn't mention. The best part by far, though, was that we had hte place entirely to ourselves, and it was so quiet and peaceful. This is what we come here for, and it's increasingly hard to find.

The wind was insane but the sun was warm and the air was warming by the minute. We loved our time on top of Cranberry Hill. Our walk back aolng the path was also very peaceful, and we even saw a garter snake out basking just south of the little bridge over the marsh. He was red-chocolatey brown and beautiful! He stared at us for a second but took off back into the weeds as soon as we reached for the camera. HE sure has a great spot here, with everything a snake could need.

on our way back into town, Rich stopped at A&B Naturals so I could pick up some crackers to enjoy with our goat cheese. They have so mcuh great stuff in that tiny store! Even crackers, just way too many to choose from. I ended up with plain Sesmark rice crackers and standard sea salt Firehook crackers, in addition to Better with Buckwheat Maine crisps (blueberry and walnut variety). I had to get them! Believe it or not, Rich even really liked the buckwheat crackers and would like to try some of their other flavors (Which they supposedly stock at Wegmans) and mayube even their plan buckwheat crackers.

We spent the rest of the afternoon reading and researching. I came across something very exciting regarding the old road/trail that we discovered. The _Pathmakers_ map seems to indicate that this was part of the Anemone Cave path (in addition to the current path) so I looked into the details for path #369:

> A popular destination in the 1800s and 1900s, but no trail marked or maintained by the VIA. Trail built by CCC in 1935 under supervision of V. Lunt. Shown on 1941 master plan map. A new trail constructed (south of old trail) by Mission 66 crew in c. i960. Terminus later closed by NPS to discourage entrance into cave (due to damage of anemones).
>
> Route: From parking area, through woods, along cliff, to cave. Current route is a paved Mission 66 trail with pipe culverts.
>
> Historic route is located to the north, with four-foot wide tread, rock lined, beginning at graveled and obscure parking lot at Schooner Head Road. 

I have a good feeling that this describes exactly what we found!

Dinner at Brasserie Le Brun was something we'd been looking forward to all year. It actually exceeded our expectations. We took a slow walk down starting just after 3:30pm, and realized that it only took 6 minutes to reach the restaurant even though we had been strolling so slowly. We had some time to peruse the menu and make a few selections before the door opened just before 4:00pm.  We were the first ones in the door, but another couple was right on our heels. This might portend another busy restaurant and loud meal, we feared, but we were wrong about that. The restaurant remained peaceful and quiet the entire time, and although a few other couples came in at various times, everyone was seated far apart from all of the other guests, so we felt like we had the place to ourselves.

One of the main things we were looking forward to was our favorite cocktail, Death in the Afternoon. Unlike last year, the server knew (or seemed to know) what we were asking for, and we were so glad that they were able to make the drink for us.

Our only real debate was what to choose for an appetizer. Everything, aside from mussels and foie gras, sounded so good! We went with the wild mushroom tart, which we've had once or twice before and we knew it to be excellent. The bread was taking a little longer than usual, but the server brought it out soon, and we indulged in tonight's offerings of plain baguette and multi-seed whole grain bread, with their awesome chive butter. We ended up ordering more bread because it was just so good! And we only had one appetizer; at least that's how we rationalized it. :)

Our entrees were even better than expected. While I can never resist the filet, this time I got it as steak frites rather than the au poivre version I usually get. And Rich had the short rib with mashed potatoes and roasted root vegetables again. They were both so tender and savory and some of the most satisfying food we've had in a while! We truly loved every bite.

We had just a bit of room left for dessert, which was a tough choice between cranberry tart and the cinnamon beignets (and the carrot cake) but Rich chose the beignets, much to my surprise, and I'm so glad he did! They were awesome too, so incredibly light and crisp and served with mascarpone, figs, and a port wine reduction. Perfect way to end the meal! I still had half of my cocktail left, too, so that was also part of my dessert. (I woudl have loved to have a second one, but I could barely stand up after the first!)

On our way back to the room, we stopped in the new hotel, Pathmakers, to see what ti was like inside and to check out the menu and atmosphere of their restaurant, the Ovens. It looks pretty decent, and wasn't at all busy this evening. Rich wasn't too excited by anything on the menu, but I'm sure we coud find something that would be to our liking. We really enjoyed looking at the historic photos on the hallway walls.
