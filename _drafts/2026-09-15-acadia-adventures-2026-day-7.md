At first I was thinking I would go for a run today on the carriage roads, but while we were discussing our options for today, Rich mentioned something about the Bear Brook picnic area, where he had found it comfortable to sit and read a few years ago when he was injured, and I went off to explore the lower reaches of Champlain and Huguenot Head. He sounded very interested in doing something similar today, and I was up for it.

After our typical breakfast of oatmeal and banana, we headed over to the picnic area, arriving right at 9:00am. Rich got settled at the only picnic table in the sun, after waiting for a man to put away an antenna and some kind of gear (I'm thinking ham radio?) and head out. I, too, took off into the woods. My loose plan was to hike the connector over to the Beechcroft trail, hike up Beechcroft to as close as I could come to the summit of Huguenot Head, and then make my way to the summit, which has no official trail. Then I would descend to the four-way intersection with the Black & White trail, and head southwest, following the faint trail and line of cairns I had found a few years ago, down to Route 3, if I could make it that far. 

My original plan was to hike back up, once I reached Route 3, and then come down the Black & White trail, checking on the steps I'd found years ago, and following it back to the picnic area. 

Everything mostly followed my plan. I hiked the connector and then up Beechcroft, satisfied that I had seemingly the entire trail to myself. I found what appeared to be a few little trails leading north from the various switchbacks, and I followed them as far as I could, thinking that either of them might have been the original path up Huguenot Head (or Picket Mountain, as it was called at the time), back when there was such a trail. Neither of those little trails amounted to anything.

I continued on, and once I got within a few hundred feet of the summit, wide bare ledges opened up in front of me, and I figured this would be the easiest access point. It was quite easy, just a few large rocks to heave myself up onto, and no brush to wade through. Soon I was standing at the summit, which offers relatively unobstructed 360 degree views of the bay, Champlain, and Dorr. At one point I thought I heard voices on Champlain, but again, I did not see a single person.

When leaving the summit, I checked the side opposite from where I had come up, thinking there might be an even easier route. I did spot one cairn, but only one, and it didn't seem to point to or originate from any easier path than the one I had taken.

The little pond at the base of Huguenot Head has completely dried up this season. There are a few aquatic grasses hanging on, and some sphagnum, but no standing water, and no frogs.

I made my way to the four-way intersection and began following the relatively well-worn path down the mountain. I found all of the cairns I had previously marked, and soon I was on my own to find more. Which I did. There was even one Bates cairn (Someone was getting clever!) But it wasn't long before I lost any sign of the trail or of cairns. I made my way down in the most logical places, and then I spotted a random cairn through the trees. I couldn't see any others from that point when I reached it, though. I pushed on, and eventually spotted a large, smooth, sloping ledge. I'm certain that both Marchon and Libby had mentioned such a ledge in this area, so I worked my way over to it, thinking I might spot a cairn on it, or near its top, that would point out the true direction of the trail. While I did find a cairn at the base of the ledge, I didn't see anything near its top.

I continued on down, through mostly open woods. The trickiest part here was the footing, since there are some loose rocks hidden beneath leaf debris, and plenty of dead branches strewn around that are perfectly designed to trip up a human crashing downhill. But otherwise it was clear sailing. I spotted random cairns here and there, but again couldn't make much sense of them. It wasn't a problem, because the woods were open and I could even see the highway below by this point. 

Then suddenly I spotted a sign on a tree. "Bear Research Area" So cool! Apparently the park service and College of the Atlantic have set up 15-20 areas with hair traps and trail cameras to study the increasing bear activity on the island. It isn't known whether there is a resident population of black bears or if they are coming over seasonally from the mainland. But there has been a significant increase in bear sightings in recent years. It was so neat to see the heavily-flagged area set up in the middle of the woods, and the obvious trail camera (that I'm sure I appear on!). 

I was nearly at the highway, and realized that I really didn't want to make my way back up and over the entire mountain again, so I changed my plan slightly to take a brisk walk up the (fortunately wide) should of Route 3, enter the bottom of the Beechcroft path, and then take it to the connector where I could walk back easily to the picnic area.  

Except I still wanted to check on those steps on the Black & White path! I had been gone exactly two hours when I reached the connector trail again and radioed Rich. I wanted him to know that I was fine and had completed the main part of my mission, but that I had one more thing I wanted to check on. That's fine, he said, go ahead!

So I made my way down the old bicycle path and to the old road, which leads to the base of the Black & White. I went basically straight up toward the steps, and found them easily, and still in great shape. It's too bad they are so hard to photograph, because they look really neat in person.

After verifying that everything still looked as I expected, I finally headed back to the picnic area. At one point I thought I'd be clever and just bushwhack the 200 feet out to the road rather than returning to the bicycle path and connector. That was going fine until I found myself in the middle of a bog with a wide-eyed whitetail deer, and no way out. It was muddy and mossy, and the grasses and cattails were over my head. Nope. So I took the long (well, slightly longer) way around, and returned to the picnic area. 

I told Rich about my adventures, and then the first things both of us said is "want to get a lobster roll?" Of course, that's what we did! Rich dropped me off at the Frenchman Bay lobster stand in town and drove around the block until I had the lobster rolls in hand. He picked me up and we brought them back to eat them on the porch.

They were so good, and just like last year, completely stuffed with lobster meat! We got two small rolls this time. I couldn't remember which size we were getting last year, but I think I chose correctly. It's good to know, too, that the large contains about 50% more meat, and on a slightly larger bun. So if we're hungrier we could get two large rolls, or for a slightly smaller portion, we could get one large roll and share it.

As usual, the rest of the afternoon was spent doing some research on the area I'd hiked. The path I'd found, or approximated, was what _Pathmakers_ refers to as #341: Huguenot Head to Otter Creek Road. My tracklog matches up pretty well with the trail as shown on the 1942 Acadia topo map, which is a good canonical source of accurate routes for most trails and roads in this area.

I didn't realize it at the time, but Marchon actually has a chapter in his second book about this trail. I think I had glossed over it because he titled it "Huguenot Falls" and I'm not a big waterfall person. Anyway, he describes mostly what I experienced, although he says that above the ledges, the trail actually goes to the right/southeast side of the streambed, and that there are stone steps there. I stayed on the northwest side of the stream and did no see any steps. So that is something to come back for!

On that 1942 topo map, I also spotted a BM near the spot where I had emerged from the woods onto Route 3. This was interesting, because I don't recall ever knowing about this. It doesn't appear on the recent topo maps. But I do have a description for it, and funny enough, on the street view I see a large rock in what appears to be the approximate location of the mark. The only wrinkle is that it was reported not found in 1971 (probably why it is not on more recent topo maps). But I do think it is worth taking a few minutes to check out that rock, just in case.

It was another Rosalie's night, and we were ready for it even after our relatively late lobster roll lunch! We didn't hurry to get there today, so we figured we wouldn't be the first ones in line. But not only were we first, there wasn't even a line to get in! I could hardly believe it. Things will change tomorrow when two cruise ships are in town (one of them having 4000 passengers) but overall so far this trip has been much, much less busy than previous years. Maybe we just hit a lull with the dates we're here, or something. But it is most welcome. Rich could hardly believe it when I told him I didn't see a single person anywhere on my 4-mile hike today.

So we had no trouble getting the first booth at Rosalie's again, and this time we tried a different beer: Baxter Coastal Haze IPA, and a regular medium pizza, which of course is our standard order when we want more than a small pizza but not as much as a medium with toppings.  It was so delicious, and the pizza came out with lots of nice bubbles in the crust this time, which we both really like.  We added a sprinkling of red pepper flakes for something a little different. The restaurant stayed strangely unbusy and quiet the whole time, which was so welcome. We had a great conversation about fonts again - this time Comic Sans (the topic of the new book Rich is reading) and about Basil Bell, an old-time typesetter and letterpress printer that Rich knew when he was a young man.

After all that pizza and beer, we decided to come straight back to the room instead of wandering through town. When we arrived here we found our next door neighbors having a little wine party on the porch with their friends who were staying on the other side of the building. They asked for a few pointers about restaurants and activities on the island, which we were happy to share with them (as long as they returned our chairs to our porch before morning, which they did!)
