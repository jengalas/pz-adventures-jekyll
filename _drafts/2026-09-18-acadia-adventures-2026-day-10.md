Survey marks: K 4 (found), K 6 (not found) W 144 - talked to someone in the driveway but the owner wasn't at home

It was a real tossup for what we were going to do this morning. We could spend some time walking around town, or explore the area around Lake Wood further, or maybe go up to Trenton to look for the benchmark K 4 and maybe get a lobster roll somewhere in that area for lunch. We made our decision over oatmeal, as usual. And better tea, since I ahd purchased a little tea infuser yesterday at A&B Naturals so I could steep some of the "Real tea" I'd bought at Beech Hill Farm.

I think the weather, which began heavily cloudy after the overnight rain, cleared up in a matter of minutes to a pure blue sky, had something to do with our ultimate decision. It was supposed to be a warm, very sunny day, and it seemed perfect for poking around and exploring, and looking for a benchmark or two. (And of course, eating lobster rolls!)

## Oak Point Road to Oak Hill Road, for benchmarks

We headed north toward Trenton, on the way briefly checking out the NGS benchmark on the Trenton bridge that we've never really even attempted to recover. It's doable, but seems pretty dangerous because there isn't a good nearby spot to park and it would require walking along a very narrow shoulder on a bridge where the traffic flies like crazy. So we skipped that and may work up the courage to try it another time.  

On our way to GOose Cove Road, we of course had to stop at the Sugar Bakery, a neat cash-only coffee and pastries spot that we discovered last year. Like last year, we ended up with a spinach and feta savory croissant, and two blueberry cream cheese croissants. We ate one of the blueberry croissants at a picnic table outside, beneath the pergola. It wasn't until I stood up that I realized that the bench must still have been wet from the rain, because my butt was damp (and stayed that way for a while). There is a little "bridge to nowhere" here that we couldn't quite figure out the purpose of, because it doesn't seem to go anywhjere in particular, and there is an easy way to cross the culvert just a few yards to the south.

We continued on, driving GOose Cove ROad to its northern end, where we expected to find USGS benchmark K 4. I had a good feeling as we approached and I could see the giant 8' x 10' boulder in the yard of th  defunct convenience store. Parked just across the road and walked toward the boulder - I could see the mark on top from yards away. No challenge, but sometimes I like them that way! We documented the mark and moved on. 

We weren't so lucky wiht the second mark we attempted, whihc basically turned out as I'd expected. IT was a second attempt at K 6, the elusive mark at the intersection of Oak Hill Road and Indian Point Road in Indian Point. I looked for it two years ago without any luck, and we had the same issure today - we're pretty sure we found the ledge, but it's so covered with moss and branches and other forest debris that we have no idea ahow we'll be able to clear it to find the mark. There are good enough references in the description that should allow us to get close, and then if we can obtain a metal detector or a pinpointer, that should tell us where to clear. Otherwise it's jsut far too daunting. At least we positivly identified the transformer pole #72 that is referred to, so we can use that with the rangefinder to narrow it down further.

There was a bit of time left before the Traveling Lobster opened at 11:30, so we drove just a bit further down Oak Hill Road to look for a tiny preserve called Prays Brook Marsh. On the map it appeared to have a very short trail into the marsh, and that's basically what it was. Maybe a hundred feet through the woods and then a short bogwalk out to a platform with a bench. It was a peaceful spot with no sounds around anywhere, other than a few frogs diving into the water here and there. The sun was hot by now, but we still enjouyed spending some time on the bench and chatting about memories and the aquatic plants we saw in front of us. No birds to be seen!

Then it was time to try the Travelin' Lobster. (Somethig about how we just discovered that the Bucking Lobster closed very early this year, on August 28. We had been planning to go there, maybe even today.) This is a cool roadside stop that has an outdoor bar with, believe it or not, a very Key West feel! We ordered two small lobster rolls (just like at the small stand in town) and a lemonade. We sat at a high-top table with an umbrella near the bar, and enjoyed the food as well as the setting. Although it was very busy, the tables were spread out enough that it didn't feel crowded, and didn't get loud (aside from the hgihway noise). We ate leisurely, saving the big claws on top of our lobster rolls to eat last. Amazing!

We went back to the room by way of Eagle Lake Road, hoping that by some chance, someone might be outside at #756 and maybe, just maybe, we could recover W 144 that we've been looking for for decades. What do you know, there was a van with the rear door open when we passed by! Wow! Rich parked the car, but soon my excitement diminished. We saw a worker up on a ladder, working on the house's siding, and it seemed pretty clear that he wasn't the owner but a hired worker. We still inquired, and he said that the owner (a woman - we were expecting it to be Phil Smith) was out at work, that she works several days a week and shoudl generally by home by 4:00pm. He said if he was still there when she got home, that he would mention we had stopped by and what we were looking for. So maybe another day this week, we'll get to try again.


Afternoon snack on the porch was more lemonade, and we shared the savory croissant of spinach and feta. Very good, just like last year!

The afternoon was spent resting and researching benchmarks again, and reminiscing about some of our old finds (and not-found marks!).

Our "light supper" was at the Ovens. We'd been going back and forth about this all week; none of the entrees really appealed to us, but the flatbreads and some of the appetizers sounded really good. We figured that tonight would be a good test to see what the atmosphere, and of course the food, is like. 

We walked in around 4:20pm to an empty restaurant (just the way we like it!) and it stayed that way almost until we were ready to leave. Our drinks were the Primrose for me (rose gin, elderberry, lemon, prosecco) and Rich had ____ ale. Clam chowder and calamari with marinara and cocktail sauce were our "appetizers" - both excellent - and then the flatbreads came out. We chose the "fig & pig" (with fig jam, mozzarella and prosciutto) and the "firebird" with chicken, hot honey, and red pepper flakes. They were light and delicious, with a perfect mix of sweet, spicy, and salty. What made them different from similar flatbreads I've had in the past was how good the bread itself was! IT was so flavorful, almost like the pizza crusts we make ourselves at home. We had absolutely no room for dessert, but we enjoyed every bite of our meal, just like all of the other goodies we had throughout the day.
