got up at 5:45, headed down to Table Salt just before 7:00am. Cold but sunny day that would warm up quickly. I had to pee so after hanging around the start line for a while (no Judy!) I took a quick warm-up run down to the public restrooms near the waterfront (for the cruisers). This did help get me loosened up and also free of pee for the whole race. I'm glad Rich suggested it.



Our breakfast (which really felt like lunch to me, even though I realized while we were eating that it was only 9:15am) was at Sunrise, thanks to a gift card from my father and Lucy. Rich ordered his favorite, the veggie lover's crepe, and I went with the banana Nutella one in honor of my father. (We've started calling it the Dave special.) The cafe was busy but we still had an enjoyable breakfast, and then we took a long, slow walk along West Street back to our room.

Our afternoon was spent recovering from the morning's activities and doing some historical research, in addition to poking around on the MaineDOT survey application to see what other survey markers we might be able to add to our list. 

Supper was a long-awaited medium house veggie pizza from Rosalie's! We had been waiting all year for another one, and it sure didn't disappoint. We walked down around 3:30 to ensure we were at or near the head of the line, and fortunately we were the first ones there. It took probably 10 minutes for the next couple to arrive, and then they began pouring in like crazy. As usual, the line went all the way down the block and wrapped around the building. The third couple in line began chatting with us. We learned that they're from Cato, NY and have been coming to MDI since 1974, so almost as long as Rich has been. They are also rabid Rosalie's fans, and they have a favorite booth (the "Jackie Gleason booth") that they like to get each time they come, for whatever reason. I think that's the main reason they showed up so early! We assured them that we had the first booth in mind, not the Jackie Gleason booth, so there would be no competition. While we were chatting, they happened to ask if we took the coastal route along Route 1, and they began talking about Wiscasset. In one of those really strange coincidences, the man mentioned these two old abandoned ships along the shore that they watched over the years decay into nothing. The odd coincidence is that we had never heard of these ships until about an hour before we left the room for dinner, when I happened to come across a mention of them in this old MaineDOT survey mark description: 
https://mdotapps.maine.gov/survey/cp_plus.html?objIDz=79810216

So strange! Anyway, they were very friendly, and they did indeed get their favorite booth (and we got the first one on the ground level, which was the one we were planning on taking today), they enjoyed their pizza, and said goodbye to us on their way out. We always have pleasant encounters with people at Rosalie's!

Tonight in addition to the house veggie pizza Rich ordered us two Baxter Staycation Lagers, which went great with the pizza! I would get it again, but the Allagash White is also so hard to resist. The pizza and conversation were perfect, and as planned, we saved two slices of pizza to bring back with us so that we could still have our usual Blondihacks pizza party, in some form. That also worked out very well, and somehow we had enough room for those remaining slices of pizza even after stuffing ourselves at the restaurant. We even, believe it or not, had enough room for a few "Puffys with Steve" afterward! His "Sidetrack Adventures" this week was about another abandoned section of Route 80 in California, the Mountain Springs section. I need to search and see if there are any benchmarks along that stretch.
