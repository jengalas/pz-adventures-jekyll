You've guessed it: breakfast was oatmeal, banana, and a blueberry muffin split between us, as usual. We sat on the porch, also as usual, and it was a much warmer morning than yesterday (although cloudy). Originally I had been thinking about going for a carriage road run, but since I did my own thing yesterday, I wanted to spend today hiking somewhere with Rich. So I suggested going back to the Somes-Meynell sanctuary to hike the longer loop, beginning at the firehouse, that we hadn't hiked last week.

{% include tracklog.html %}

On our way back to the room, we took a detour (not a short one, since we basically had to drive all the way around the island from Beech Hill) to check out that rock that I thought might have held the benchmark K 21. I worked my way up to it, slipping and sliding on the steep, pine needle-covered slope, and immediately I could see that there was no benchmark on the exposed portion of the rock. It sure looked like a great rock for a benchmark, though, and it matches the description. At least half of the rock is covered with a thick layer of pine needles, so it's possible that the mark is hiding beneath them. It would require a trowel or small shovel to clear the rock, and it's a rather precarious spot, so it would have to be done with care. But it still might be worth a try.

When we drove away, I noticed another nice large rock just a very short distance north that might also be a good candidate. If we drive by there again, I will check it out.

Our little lunchtime snack was the two scones we'd bought at Beech Hill Farm eaten with the remainder of the goat cheese (and some of the crackers, just to finish off the cheese). The scones were so good! The cheddar, chive, and dill one was very flavorful and savory, and went perfect with the goat cheese. The rose cardamom scone was delicious and so unusual, and just very slightly sweet.  It also went well with the goat cheese, but I think I preferred that one on its own.

Dinner at Red Sky: we arrived around 4:50 after narrowly getting through a long traffic backup on Route 102. We were the first to arrive, but soon afterward other diners began queuing up at the door. The menu looked similar to usual with a few updates, as always. Rich was pretty much set on getting my father's favorites in honor of him: crispy polenta with mushrooms, and the roasted half chicken. I was torn between the sole and the tagliatelle. I ended up with the tagliatelle with added smoked salmon. I had been planning to get the smoked salmon appetizer, but instead I was drawn by the Great Hill blue cheese salad with dried cherries and balsamic vinaigrette, so I chose to have the salmon added to the pasta dish. That was an excellent choice! I wasn't actually sure if the flavors would go well together, so I was prepared to eat the pasta and salmon separately if necessary. But they made a great combination.

The polenta and the salad were also excellently prepared. Honestly, although Red Sky is always good, everything we had tonight exceeded my expectations. Rich's chicken was perfect, so well seasoned and cooked till the skin was crisp but the meat was still so juicy inside. The whole cranberry sauce was tart but slightly sweet, and, well, roasted potatoes in any form are always my favorites. The pasta was as delicious as ever, mixed with Swiss chard, and topped with ricotta, lemon, and Aleppo pepper (and of course accompanied by the salmon). 

We also really enjoyed our drinks: wheat beer for Rich and the "Summertime" cocktail for me (gin, grapefruit juice, St. Germain, and Gran Marnier) which I had a variation of last year, and found it so refreshing.

And somehow after all of that, we had room for dessert. And not just one dessert shared between us, as usual, but one apiece. Rich went for the special, a light lemon mousse cake served over blackberry syrup, while I had their famous gingerbread. Even that was somehow better than I remember it from previous years.
