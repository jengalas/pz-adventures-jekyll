Carriage road (Witch Hole Connector carriage road) to crossover to Breakneck road, then Breakneck Road north to the spot at the northern end of the northern pond where we head to the boulder - along the old road. Found steps/stepping stones crossing Breakneck Brook at this point; unsure what they are from. Then came back out toward Breakneck road, where I set off to find the steps from the old Fawn Pond path using coordinates I had from GPSAcadia.

We didn't even have to discuss what we wanted to do this morning, over our oatmeal and banana and phyllo cigars left over from last night. Just for the record they reheated beautifully in the microwave and somehow were still crispy! Really tasty for breakfast. We both knew that we wanted to walk the carriage road from the Eagle Lake parking area toward the Breakneck Ponds, cross over to Breakneck ROad, and check out the boulder that used to be one of Rich's favorite private spots that he would ride to on his bike. Then we were going to poke around looking for any sign of the old road on the other side of the brook that we see on the old maps (the road we take to the boulder from Breakneck Road is part of this old road, but Rich didn't recall it continuing across the brook). We wanted to see if we could locate any part of it on the western side of the brook. Then, if there was time, I also wanted to search for the steps and stepping stones that are relics of the old Fawn Pond path. 

I was expecting we'd park on the road shoulder near the Eagle Lake carriage road parking lot, but we checked the lot first and a spot just so happened to open up at the right time. Our slow walk north along the Witch Hole Connector carriage road brought us first to the marshy outflow south of the Breakneck Ponds, where we stepped off the carriage path for a bit and found another wonderful world of mushrooms! They are literally everywhere this year. I looked in vain for sundews here (but there is still more to come along those lines). 

As we continued north along the carriage road, we saw more, and more, and more mushrooms everywhere we looked! Simply amazing. Rich stopped to admire and photograph quite a few of them. Meanwhile, I was taking a littel trail to reach the southernmost point of the south Breakneck Pond. Well, I sure found my fill of sundews there! It was the motherlode. The muddy shore faded into sphagnum, all of it entirely covered with _Drosera rotundifolia_. I was afraid to walk farther because there simply was nowhere to stand without stepping on sundews.

Back on the carriage road, we continued north to the crossover point between the carriage road and Breakneck Road. We followed Breakneck road north for a short distance until we reached the intersection of the old road that would lead us to Rich's spot. The road is still doubletrack and several yards wide along most of its length, but it is much more overgrown than it was 20 years ago. And one short section just before the pond issignificantly overgrown. We had to fight our way through laurel and blueberry bushes. FOrtunately, that section was only a few yards long, and we soon popped out at the northern end of the pond.

I found some more sundews here, and while Rich worked his way over to the boulder, I worked my way through the woods in teh opposite direction to see if I could find a way to cross the brook, intent on learnig if there was any trace of the old woods road on the other side. I"m still not sure about that, but I did find a set of steps or stepping stones climbing the short hill just to the east of the brook! And what do appear to be stepping stones/stone pavers laid across the brook itself, right where it flows into the pond.

A path does indeed continue across the brook, but it is narrow and looks more like a social trail (or even a deer trail) than remnants of an old road. I followed it for a short distance, stopping and turning around at a distinct boulder that made a good landmark for future explorations. I'm not sure yet what this is or was, and whether it goes any further than I was able to take it today. I didn't see any obvious continuation, but sometimes I'll lose track of these old roads for a while, but then pick them up again if I just keep moving in the approximately correct direction. We can check it out again in the future, particularly during another dry year.

I joined Rich at the boulder, where I found another large area of sundews. Then we decided that I would try working my way through the woods toward the steps from the old Fawn Pond path. The coordinates I had were only .15 miles away. We set up our radios, and I set off. This is a truly beautiful area of woods! It's all moss covered and maybe boggy during a wetter year, but open woods and soft ground cover made for perfect walking today. And it was probably my imagination, or just a game trail made by deer beelining to the brook for a drink, but I swear that most of the way I felt like I was following a path. Unlikely as that may be - it really doesnt' make sense that any features other than stone or metal would survive first after the 1947 fire and secondly after decades of disuse. But it was a pleasant, easy walk, and I was soon at the brook.

I worked my way down the slippery moss-covered rocks, and before I knew it, I was standing on a stretch of stepping stones. They continued to lead me down and around the ledges along the eastern side of the brook. I had found the steps! 

If I'd had more time, I would have loved to try following the brook north another quarter mile or so to the place where it crosses the road, and look for whatever other set of steps MArchon supposedly found. I didn't have enough time for that today, but I will keep it in mind, and I'm going to pull a few sets of coordinates from the topo map to give me a few logical places to aim for.

Back on Breakneck Road after a little celebration of the find (Rich was really glad that I had solved one of my little "mysteries") we retraced our steps to the parking area. What a great day out together, and a truly beautiful and exciting walk it was!

We took another slow stroll down to Havana around 3:40 for our 4:00 reservation. I was expecting that they'd have the fall menu in place by now, but it was still the summer menu. Not that it's a problem - we really liked everything we had last week, and at first we were thinking about getting the same things again. But then we took a closer look at the other menu items, and I was really drawn to the lobster moqueca. It contains all of my favorite things: lobster, haddock, red pepper, pineapple, cilantro, and a coconut curry broth over jasmine rice. How did I not try this before?! It's always on the menu as one of their two "flagship" entrees. I think it's because unless it's whole lobster or a lobster roll from a lobster pound, I typically gloss over the lobster entrees on a menu, thinking that they're probably mainly tourist-trap items. But I had to try this. Rich took a close look at the preparation for the filet and decided to get that tonight. So we both had something new to try (Rich had, in fact, ordered the lobster moqueca once before, but many years ago.). 

Our amuse bouche was two cucumber rounds topped with peach glaze, prosciutto bits, and a balsamic glaze. NExt came our traditional mojitos. Similar to last Sunday, the bread was blueberry cornbread and slices from a plain Italian loaf, this time served with rosemary-infused olive oil, roasted garlic hummus, and a cinnamon maple butter that Rich really liked. As always, we got the spring roll appetizer - they are so satisfying, and we could eat a dozen or more of them (as we always say). The entrees were really spectacular too. The lobster moqueca was even better than I expected, with a rich, sweet broth and a lot more lobster meat and fish than I would have thought. The filet was perfectly cooked, and although I was initially hesitant about the espresso rub on the outside, I didn't even taste coffee at all, just a perfect char-grilled taste. The filet came with simple veggies and fondant potatoes, in addition to the tiniest pearl onions you can imagine. So good! And for dessert, just like last week, we got the Havana martini (with pineapple) and the guava parfait again.

We took a slow walk back through town, partly because we were enjoying the walk, the weather conditions, the company and the scenery, and partly because we were full of good food and drink. We laughed along with two girls who were trying to rediscover the lost art of tree climbing, and not getting far (but at least making a good attempt). 

Another cozy evening was spent looking at our photos and tracklogs, working on notes, and watching a few entertaining videos before bed.
