Quick trip to Hannaford in the morning after breakfast of oatmeal, coffee/tea and juice, yogurt, and blueberry muffin. Then I heard back from the woman at MDI Historical Society, who said it would be fine if I dropped the book off at the Somesville museum, which was open today from 10-4 (only open Fridays at this time of year). In fact Rich and I were just talking about this at breakfast and thinking it was a possibility for both dropping off the book and visiting the museum for the first time. 

Hannaford was busy, but not nearly as bad as it will be this evening, so I was glad that we stopped there this morning for some drinks, vitamins, and other minor needs. Watermelon air freshener - who knew? It smells pretty nice.

After dropping off our purchases in the room, we drove over to Somesville and parked in the museum lot. Immediately I liked the calm feeling of the place, even though we were far from the only ones there. A few other groups were already poking around the gardens. They soon left, and we mostly had the place to ourselves. A woman came to raise the flag. We noticed an accurate sundial, and wondered how they adjust it back to standard time, when the time comes. (Or maybe they don't, because the museum is only open June through October?)

The little museum had more exhibits than I was expecting. The well designed posters cover the area's history from the Wabanaki through to the present. A few maps and other artifacts are featured, along with interpretive text on the posters. Our favorite item was the Somes House Inn register and the fantastical and hilarious drawings contained within. The page featured today was captioned "What Mrs. Whatz saw after eating XIII pop-overs" and showed a drawing of a monster-faced, winged creature (a popover?) flying directly at the viewer. We asked the museum staffer about this, and she laughed and told us about the other great drawings in the book, one of which is titled "The Pop-over's Revenge" and shows a giant popover eating tiny people. They don't like to handle the book too often, which makes sense, but she brought up the image in their digital archive, and it really was so bizarrely hilarious! The woman (Jennifer) working there said that they really don't know the origin of the drawings, but they suspect it may have been a young summer hire with an excess of energy and chutzpah (my word, not hers). Some of them supposedly depict the adventures various guests had during their time staying at the Inn. While there, we also spent time browsing the books for sale and I bought the brand new book by Catherine Schmitt, Trees of Acadia.

After going through all of the exhibits at the museum, we walked over the famous bridge to the Selectmens House. This is a truly tiny building, built around 1780. There aren't many exhibits here, mostly just some old photographs of the town (but at least one shows the Selectmens House building!). But the best part was the giant wall hanging of the 1887 Colby & Stuart map of the island. I would love to have one, if only I had the room for it!

We thought we might also poke around in the library across the street, but we found out that they're only open on Wednesdays and Saturdays. So we moved on to our next activity for the day, which was to check out the short trails at the Somes-Meynell Wildlife Sanctuary that we had spotted on the map. 

The map indicates a parking area right at the intersection of ROute 102, but the only thing there was the fire station, where we parked two years ago to search for a benchmark. It didn't seem like this could be the right spot, so we continued on to look for the other parking area that the map indicated farther to the west. We thought we found it when we saw a generously sized gravel pulloff and an old wooden gate with what appeared to, maybe, be a trail heading into the woods. Our thought was further confirmed when we saw a small trail marker indicating that this was sanctuary land on a tree.

Whatever we thought was a trail, though, apparently was just our imaginations making patterns out of basically open woods. I knew we had to be cliose, though, so I wandered around until I came across the trail, and then went back to get Rich. We followed the trail west .. right to a nice parking area and two buildings that were apparently the headquarters for the sanctuary. 

THe headquarters building was closed, but it was clear that a lot of activity takes place here. There was equipment stored for volunteers doing amphibian tracking, and a sheet tracking loon nesting sites all over the island. We headed down the steps adjacent to the hq building, intending to follow the Nature Trail. It was peacful right along the shore of the pond, soft footing and beautiful view of the pond and a small island in it. 

Soon we heard voices in the near distance. At first we thought they were other hikers, but when we came across them it was immediatley clear that they were working on something related to the sanctuary. We enjoyed a great conversation with the men (named Bill, Rusty, Greg, and Stanley - the Somesville Beatles?!) and learned that they were setting up "floating classrooms" for a school group coming on Thursday. They told us about the loon nesting observations and the other work they do as part of the sanctuary and the Gulf of Maine Research Institute. Aparenttly there are nature walks and other activities offered by the sanctuary, so they told us how to sign up for their mailing list so we'll find out what's happening. We also asked about snakes (they assumed at first that we were looking to avoid snakes, but son learned that no, we were looking to find them!). They knew of some unfortunately private sites where they often see snakes basking, including near swimming pools. They listed garters, ringnecks, red bellies, and occasional milk snakes as the most commont osee on the island. We've seen all of them here, other than the milk snakes.

We completed our hike, just doing the short nature trail loop and planning to come back soon to complete the full loop, including the spur to and from the fire station. We also wanted to check out the other secion of the sanctuar across the pond, that also has a short trail. We drove around to find it, and this time pretty easily located the proper parking spot adn the trail. But we will save it for another day.

After all these adventures it was time to head back to the room for a bit of rest and a banana. Then I took a jog down to the YMCA to pick up my race bib for tomorrow morning. It wasn't too busy and I only had to wait a few minutes. Jogged back with it and spent some time doing the usual local history research before geting ready for Dinner.

I wanted to be at Dinner for 5:00pm, the time they open, just in case - although the reservation they made for us was for 5:15. There was noproblem being seated when we walked in rihgt at 5:00. We got our usual front table, which was perfect (once they came and removed the fly trap papers). We started with drinks, Italian style Pils for R and a margarita for me, both of which really hit the spot. Then we had the scallop appetizer. Honesly all of the appetizers sounded incredibly delicous, and Rich really wanted the scallops, so we went for it. They were awesome. So tender and served with onions and perfectly crisply cooked bacon pieces on top (much better than being bacon wrapped!) Then our entrees, pumpkin seed and panko crusted halibut and the pork schnitzel, which we had last year and was amazing. Both were setved with veggies including bok choy. The halibut had polenta and lobster sauce (with pieces of lobster) and the pork came with mashed potatoes with chives. SO good. We even had a tiny bit of room left for dessert, and thankfully (or maybe not) they had the same awesome carrot cake we had last year. We had to get it. Just as good as expected, and not too fillign since we were sharing it. The only negatives were the very loud people next to us, including a guy with the sharpest, loudest, most annoying laugh you can imagine, and two babies that were brought in for some reason. Not the place for them.

Anyway, it was nearly 7:00pm when we wandered back to the room and basically collapsed, doing a bit of research and reading before bed.
