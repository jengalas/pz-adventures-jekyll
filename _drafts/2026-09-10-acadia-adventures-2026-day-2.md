After overnight showers, the day quickly turned sunny (and hot, and remained very humid). We woke just before 7:00am after a difficult night, especially for Rich who couldn't find a comfortable position. I too thought I was tossing and turning for most of the night, but Rich said it sounded like I was sleeping well, and my watch recorded 6 and a half hours of light sleep, so it may have been better than I thought.

Just like last year, we decided to escape the interstates early and we joined Route 1 at Freeport, at the odd little t-intersection that basically dumps you off of I-295 with 200 feet to slow down for a stop sign. Not a problem, but just weird.

We had an uneventful drive to Wiscasset, where we headed directly to Churchill Street and the 1813 powder house (also a landmark station!) that we first learned about last year on our first day in Maine - unfortunately after we were already in our hotel room that evening, not while we were still in Wiscasset. Not to worry, we put it on the list for this year! And what makes this historic landmark even more exciting is the triangulation station set on the same knoll! We were very excited to search for it.

We found convenient parking in a grassy clearing just south of the powder house, and we hiked up the little knoll, made of large granite outcrops, toward it. Immediately we saw (of course) the brick cylinder and topping cone of the powder house, and between us and it, RM 1. So cool! Even more exciting was that I glanced toward the south and spotted the triangulation station on another outcrop, and then RM 2 on an outcrop immediately in front of the powder house door. We had a great time at this spot, taking photos and thinking about why this spot was chosen for the triangulation station. We also really liked the old stone steps leading between the road and the powder house, and the sign for it on the road shoulder.

After this exciting find, we were feeling a bit hungry, so we decided to find a place to sit and eat the baklava we'd picked up last night at the diner, planning to save it for this morning's breakfast (minus the two bites I'd sampled last night). Convenient parking was found right along Route 1 at Sprague's Lobster, another place we'd love to try if only we were ever in town as late as 11:00am (more on that later). This worked out well! We sat on a bench at Sprague's because the tables were still wet from the overnight rain. (Most of the benches were too, but we found one that had apparently been exposed to the sun all morning and was mostly dry.) We munched on the very sticky, but very delicious (filled with nuts!) baklava while various vendors and employees walked by delivering goods to the lobster shack. They were all very friendly.

While we ate, I mentioned the other nearby benchmarks in town. I was just going to run up to the bridge to look for the one on the western end, since it's a not-too-exciting Maine DOT mark, but Rich was game to come along too. We found the mark easily, and interestingly it is halfway hanging off the sidewalk it's set into because the concrete underneath part of it has been damaged somehow, or worn away.

After this one, we were interested in another one down by the pier on Water Street. The only problem is that you can't turn left out of Sprague's parking lot onto Route 1, which you need to be able to do to access the pier. So we ended up going east on the bridge, parking at a motel on the other side so we could locate the eastern MaineDOT mark on the bridge (which we did, easily) and then drive back west on the bridge. Left turns onto Water Street aren't permitted either, so we went up one block to Middle Street, which worked out fine to take us down to the area of the pier.

We found this mark, an NOS tidal station disk set in 2015, easily on the pier abutment. These marks can sometimes be tricky because the coordinates are often very generic and not given for each mark individually (this is why the "disgruntled surveyor" from years ago was having such a hard time of it.)

We then decided to take a walk west toward the tidal lagoon where there is supposed to be an outcrop holding another mark in this tidal set. There is a nice outcropping in the right area, and Rich even pointed out what looks like a stake or a rod on it, but unfortunately today the tide was too high and the ledge was surrounded by water. Maybe next year we will luck out with the tides and be able to reach it.

For some reason, we hadn't checked the time and still assumed it was quite early in the morning - far too early to lunch at Red's or Sprague's. It was only when we passed Red's and Rich commented that the line was arleady forming that we looked at the clock and realized that it was 10:50! The line only had 10-15 people in it, so this would have been a perfect opportunity to try the famous lobster shack (or Sprague's, which also opens at 11:00)! But by the time we put all of this together, we were already back on the bridge heading east, and traffic heading west was at a standstill. It would have taken quite a while to get back to town, find a plce to park, and get into the line (whic by then would certainly be much longer). So we continued on to Young's but I'm still kicking myself for not looking at the time after finding that last benchmark! Next year!!!

Even getting to Young's was a bit of an adventure. Route that looked like it was taking us on the little shortcut on 90 to Camden but we ended up going through all these little rural back roads. At least we finally got to Youngs around 12:15, and we expected it to be very busy, but it wasn't at all. We had our choice of very windy tables, and our lobster rolls were ready within about 5 minutes. Great as always! Just SO windy at that time. 

Then continued on maknig our way to Bar Harbor. No real problems here, also remember trying Court Street rather than the goofy hill/stop sign that always gets backed up on the way into Ellsworth.

Arrived at the hotel at 2:30. Larger table in place thanks to christopher! Constance checked us in. Nothiong much new to report other than Siren and Sailor, and 15% discount at the Ovens restaurant. 

Got cleaned up and went for pizza as always! Got there around 4:30 and Surprisingly not busy at all. We were able to get a booth on the first floor. Got our usual medium plain and two large Allagash Whites. Excellent pizza, company, and conversation as always! Quick stop at Hannaford on the way back for paper towels and toilet paper.

Talked to Christopher in the evening after getting more lemonade. Nothing much new from him either, except that Stephen Costen (who now owns the motel) has bought the CHerystones block and Cherrystones is now a pancake house. He also offered the Ovens discount (just don't go there for lobster!) and didn't think that Maureen had done anything with the food shack on the way to SWH. She's such a great chef, and artist really, but she just has trouble focusing on the business side of things! ALso the story abuot the larger table and how once people saw it everyone wanted one, and how he ended up buying them for all the rooms in our building. NO one had ever suggsted it before! They will firever be known as the Galas tables. (Ooh! Naming rights - can we get little bronze plaques? No, but I'll give you a SHarpie.)
