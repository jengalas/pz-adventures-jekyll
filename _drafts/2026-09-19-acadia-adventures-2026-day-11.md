Survey marks found: JDR/PVT, W 144(!!!!!!)

This morning was a chilly 49 degrees, and we weren't in a big hurry to head out to the trails. I knew it would warm up eventually, but we had a few thigns to take care of in town first. After breakfast of oatmeal, banana, and the remaining blueberry croissant from yesterday, we went to the ATM at the bank for some more cash, then to Hannaford for paper towels, and to see if they had any of the Sunset Acres Farm goat cheese. (They didn't.) We also stopped at A&B Naturals to see if they might have gotten any of the goat cheese in since the last time I was there, but they didn't have any either. It was fun looking around the store with Rich, though. And I'm still thinking I'd like to buy that Masienda blue corn meal.

When we returned from our little shopping trip, I went to teh hotel lobby to ask for the benchmark datasheet for W 144 to be printed. For some reason I didn't have a printout of that datasheet, and I thought it would be a good idea to keep it in the car in case we ended up stopping to search for it again. That wasn't a problem; Rachel at the front desk was more than happy to print it for me.

Then I proposed my ideas for today. I wanted to check out that path or road I'd found years ago in the Lake Wood/Fawn Pond area but had never followed. I could do it either from Breakneck ROad or from Lake Wood; I let Rich decide. It was so sunny that it wasn't going to be a good day for photography, so he decided that Lake Wood would be better because at least there would be a sunny spot where he could sit and read. This worked out well! I knew the trails and got to Fawn Pond quickly. I was excited to see a very large and healthy population of sundews right at the edge of the pond. I couldn't spot any pitcher plants this time, though. And just for future reference, I tried testing our radios at this spot (and at many other spots throughout my exploration) and they didn't work anywhere. So this is not a good spot to rely on them. Phone reception seems to be OK, though, except down in the Breakneck gulch.

I passed Fawn Pond and made my way quickly to the old road/trail. I followed it for a good distance and it remained pretty obvious. I was excited about where it was going to lead, even more so when I spotted a single rock cairn on a small boulder, and hten a few pieces of blue flagging tape on trees beyond. I kept going, although I was already losing the trail by this point, and came across a tree with two wraps of blue flagging tape on it, and a sign saying NPS Bird Study. Hmm! So this was probably the reason for the cairn and blue flagging leading to this point.

Sadly, that was the last I saw of this trail, aside from possibly a few sections of "something" traillike that I came across as I wandered around in the relatively open woods (nice for walking, but it makes following old roads and trails pretty challenging). I saw that I was only .6 miles from the summit of Young's Mountain and, thinking that the old road might have led up there, I briefly entertained the idea of trying to reach the summit. But with extremely poor GPS recetion in those woods, and the huge bog I came across as I made my way (I think) toward Young's, I decided that this wasn't a good idea for today, and I turnred back. It didn't take too long (althouhg longer than it should have due to the poor satellite reception) to get back to the main "road" between Lake Wood and Breakneck.

I still had some time, so I decided to walk down the road about .2 mile to check out a point I had labeled as "potential trail." I reached it and followed what did in fact appear to be a trail. But this one, too, petered out into nothing. There were no cairns spotted along the way, either, which is probably a good sign that no one is actuvely using it at this time. But it had to have been something, at some point. (I noticed later when looking at my tracklog that it may actually have connected straight through to whatever trail I was on earlier.)

And I still had a bit of time, so I thought I would jog down the rest of the road to reach Breakneck Road and then go south a short distance to the point where the road crossed Breakneck Brook. According to Marchon, there are supposed to be steps visible here from the old Fawn Pond path. I've looked for them before withotu any luck. Same story today. I want to look again and give it a lot more time, and maybe even ask Matthew if he has coordinates. I would love to see them. 


Still no steps, but I happened to look to my left and what did i spot but a BENCHMARK on top of a random boulder in the middle of Breakneck Brook! I cuold hardly believe it. Although time was growing short, I had to go over and document it. Then instead of making the backtrack on the long switchback, I took the short but steep route straight up the bank, back to the road between Lake WOod and Breakneck.

I called Rich at this point, once I had cell service back, to let him know I was on my way back. And I made pretty good time, since I'mn already familiar with these roads and trails. I stopped again at Fawn Pond just to see if this time I could spot a pitcher plant, which I know we've seen here in teh past, but I didn't see any today. Just so many sundews! I ran most of the path back to teh parking area, and Rich was just putting his things back into the car when I greeted him. Great exploration! And it sounded like he had a nice morning reading and relaxing, too.


We finally found the benchmark that has eluded us for decades! After I finished my hike at Lake Wood, Rich asked if there was anything else I wanted to do this afternoon. I suggested taking a drive by #756 Eagle Lake Road, just in case the property owner was home, and he was game for that. I was so glad that I had printed out the datasheet this morning, just in case!

It just so happened that a green Forester was in the driveway this afternoon. It wasn't there yesterday, so we assumed it was probably the homeowner's vehicle, and that she was home. We parked and went to the door. The woman who answered, Mrs. Smith, seemed a bit skeptical at first (I would be too!) and didn't seem to know anything about the benchmark. We showed her the datasheet indicating that JRO had spoken with Phil Smith 10 years ago. She commented that he had passed away in February (which I had honestly feared was the case) and that while he may have known about the marker, she had no idea what it was or where it could be. But she was more than willing to let us search.

We headed straight to the back yard, and found the foot bridge still intact, although becoming overgrown. This was a good refernece, because according to JRO, the disk was only 9 feet away from the southeast corner of hte bridge - a pretty easy distance to work with. We found a nice outcropping ledge at just about the right distance, and we started to remove the thick moss, soil, and pine needle covering. It took us a minute to realize that we had already uncovered the disk! It was right at the top of the ledge, just embedded a bit and still covered in mud. We cleaned it off as thoroughly as possible, took our photos, and headed back to show Mrs. Smith the photo. She seemed mildly interested and we thanked her again for her time and permission. I hope she goes out to check on the mark! She should be able to spot it quite easily.

The afternoon was spent doing more historical research on the trails around Fawn Pond and Lake Wood. I think the next thing we want to investigate is the road leading west from the point where I turned off onto the old road/trail today. Rich thinks that it might be the way he reached Lake Wood all those years ago, when he climbed the old road from Breakneck Road and ended up at the south end of Lake Wood, looking toward the large rocks at the north end. It might be best to approach via the western shore of Lake Wood, a path we attempted to follow a few years ago, and continue south to see what we might be able to find. I remember that we had trouble following the path past a certain point, but it should be less than a half mile down to the intersection of roads, at least according to the map, so maybe we can push our way through if necessary.

It's Blondihacks Pizza Party Saturday, and this time we wanted to order the pizza for takeout and bring it back to our room. I called as soon as they opened at 4:00pm, and they told us the pizza would be ready in 25-30 minutes. We figured we'd leave a little earlier so we could be sure to get the pizza as soon as it came out of the oven. Rich drove me down and dropped me off in front of the restaurant. Surprisngly it wasn't busy inside - there was really no line, and there were a few downstairs tables and booths open. Seemed strange for a Saturday. Anyway, I waited only about 5 minutes and the pizza was ready. I remembered to get a token, and also got some paper plates, which were a great help because the only other plates we have are about 4 inches across and won't really work for medium pizza slices.

Our mushroom pizza was so delicious, and covered with mushrooms! I like how they're sliced so thin and get nice and crispy and dry, too. It was just perfect to watch Blondihacks (and, later, South Main Auto and Sip & Feast) with the pizza and a glass of wine. And later, three cups of herbal tea because I was feeling very thirsty and a bit chilled.
