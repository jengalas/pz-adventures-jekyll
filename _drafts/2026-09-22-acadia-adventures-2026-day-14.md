Today was mostly a rest day, basically decided for us by the fact that I had a telehealth call for my father at 1:00pm, and the fact that I really needed to go shopping in town for some souvenirs and thank you gifts for the people who have been helping me out while we're away.

Our typical breakfast was a bit delayed because we spent at least 30 minutes talking with a man who was sitting on the front porch near the lobby when we went to get our breakfast. Turns out he is from Danville, PA and is the father of someone who was an anchor on our local TV station for years. He talked our ears off! But we enjoyed it since he is also a history fanatic who has been visiting MDI for 30 years now and seems to know a lot about the area and its history and famous residents.

We ate inside because it was so chilly, and then headed out for our walk downtown. Passing by the Atlantic Brewing Company, Rich noticed that the sandwich board outside said "$25 lobster rolls!" He seemed very interested in this so we kept it in mind for later. After spending quite a while evaluating socks in Cadillac Mountain Sports, we walked up and down Main Street, picking up some various jams, honeys, and teas for people and also a slice of blueberry pie for us at Downeast Deli. I'd had a craving for blueberry crepes at Sunrise, but the line was literally snaking around the entire restaurant and out the door. Not today, I'm afraid!

The lobster rolls were still calling our name, so we stopped at the brewing company on the way back to our room and got two to go. STill chilly outside, so we ate them indoors again. They were understandably a bit smaller than the ones we'd bought for $34 but still well stuffed with lobster meat. And these seem to be the "traditional" style in the top-split buns with grilled and buttered sides, a style that I don't think we've actually had before. They were a really nice change of pace, especially with a lemon to squeeze on top too.

I had the phone call from 1:00-3:00, and Rich didn't want to intrude so he sat outside reading. That would have been perfect, except it was still a chilly day in the shade (and breezy) so he got quite chilled. When I finished the call, I went outside to find him standing in the sun on the sidewalk, trying to warm up.

We hadn't been planning on another big supper, but we ended up with one anyway (and enjoyed every bite)! All day we'd been going back and forth between another dinner at Brasserie le Brun and checking out Paddy's for a hamburger and Guinness. By the time my phone call was over, Rich was feeling hungry again (and I was getting to that point too) and we made a last-minute decision to go to Le Brun again. Just can't resist that Green Fairy!

Speaking of the green fairy, tonight's server didn't know what Death in the Afternoon was, but fortunately the bartender did. This time we were presented with a list of options for the absinthe, and since we didn't know which one (or ones) we'd had before, we asked for the greenest one. That turned out to be Tree Spirits, the local Maine offering, and it was great. Strong and beautiful, and herbily delicious! Bubbles rose in a column from the stems of the glasses, providing a truly supernatural effect.

Rich was still chilled from sitting outside for two hours, so he was looking for something warming. The onion soup gratinee was just the thing. We were so pleased when they offered to split it up into two tiny crocks. This was so thoughtful and made for much easier eating. It was so good! The combination of melted cheeses was so good, we are even considering trying it on a pizza (Muenster, Gruyere, Raclette). And the onions and broth were delicious. As always we thoroughly enjoyed the baguette and chive-onion butter. 

At some point while we were eating, Deb from the Black Friar came in to sit at the bar with two men; I don't know who they were (not her husband who owns the inn and pub along with her). She had red wine and oysters. Not that it's that important, but for some reason I seem to run into her all over town, more than anyone else. This is the second time we've seen her this trip. Maybe it's just because she is pretty recognizable with her multicolored hair? I'm not sure but it is always quite amusing when we spot her.

Our second appetizer was the crab cake, which was huge and basically all crab meat with a very light seasoning. Entrees this evening were both new to us: halibut with lardons and wheat berries, and mushroom gnocchi with the addition of two huge scallops. Everything was understatedly delicious and cooked perfectly. The restaurant was quiet and peaceful, and we loved every minute. We were far too stuffed for dessert!

The rest of the evening was filled with interesting conversation and wonderment about what the weird floating thing was on the ceiling tile above the bed. (We still don't know!)
