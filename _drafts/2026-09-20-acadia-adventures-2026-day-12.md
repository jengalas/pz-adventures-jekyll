NPS boundary disk near Wild Iris Farm: 44.411600, -68.274416

I was expecting a dreary morning with rain to come later, so I wasn't even thinking we would be doing much poking around the woods, if any. But after our typical breakfast (eaten indoors today because it was 45 degrees when we woke up) we decided that it was nice enough to attempt some more poking around the area of Lake Wood. 

We were going to attempt to follow our track from 2022 and work our way down the western side of the lake, to see if by some chance we might be able to reach the other end of the road that comes up from BReakneck Road, that Rich had followed to Lake WOod all those years ago.

With the tracklog(s) ready in my GPS, we drove to the lower lot at Lake Wood and began to make our way toward the memorial bridge. For some reason we thought this was the way we had crossed to the other side of the lake, but it was impossible to do so due to the deep water and only a few logs to cross over on. I probably could have done it, altouhg it was very sketchy, but I didn't even want Rich to attempt it. The coolest thing I saw here, in addition to the old bridge and memorial stone, was a big sundew growing right out of one of the logs! I'm bummed that I wasn't able to get a photo, but the area was quite precarious and I couldn't reach far enough to frame it.

At this point we checked tjhe tracklog and realized that this was not how we had crossed to the other side of the lake last time. We had instead taken the easy, wide old road from just past the parking area west toward Wild Iris Farm, and then branched off to cross the stream at the lake's northern head. So we did the same this time, and found it much easier walking. We crossed the stream and then began heading up, straight up, on the obvious trail, not realizing how far away we were getting from the shoreline, and not rememebring that we should really be almost hugging the shore at this point. We found a large network of trails in this area that we soon realized, due to the condition of their tread, were horse trails (perhaps from the nearby horse farm?). I even found another NPS boundary marker at the far reaches of the park. These trails were interesting because they were new to us, and we followed their labyrinth for a while, hoping they might lead us down toward the lake, but whatever paths we were on just kept diverging toward the southwest, away from the lake.

It was at this point that I realized that we should have taken a hard left right after crossing the stream.  We retraced our steps and found the correct trail riht away - the one we took last time, and the one we should have taken this time. But now righted, we continued on, easily following the faint path, and finding so many awesome mushrooms along the way (this has been the best year, by far, for mushrooms that I've ever seen here. The number and variety are astounding, especially since it seems like it has been dry lately.)

We continued along, passing the last point that we'd made it to last time but unfortuantelyt not getting much farther. THe rote near the shore was basically impassable, and the route higher up was yet again taking us away from the lake. (Although based on a really great aerial image from 1956 that I found later in the afternoon, this may indeed have been an old path that, if we could continue to follow it, would still take us around to one of the points we'd hoped to reach. But we'll see.)

I had in my GPS a waypoint from GPSAcadia from 15 years ago that indicated a few steps from the old path down near the water. They appeared to be in the section of a very high imposing rock wall with talus at its base. I made my way down to it very carefully, and picked my way along the talus on the shore. I eventually found the steps! This was awesome. But this was also a very tricky spot to navigate. There was really no way to pick a path any farhter along the shore, so I carefully pulled myself up and over the talus and then the cliff face to get back into the woods, and to rejoin Rich back on the trail where we'd come in. 

When we left Lake Wood, Rich said that there was some reason he wanted to go back via Eagle Lake Road, although he couldn't remember at the time what the reason was. "Ah!" he said when we neared the Eagle Lake carriage road parking lot. IT was the entrance to Breakneck ROad, on the southern end. We had been trying to recall last night if the road was still gated, and if there was still a sign saying that snowmobiles are allowed on the road. I couldn't see it on the street view, so we had to check it out in person. And yes, Rich's memory was correct. There is still a gate with the sign, just as he'd thought.

We drove back to town via the YMCA, hoping to catch the last of the vendors at the farmers market, even though ti was already 12:20 and the market closes at noon. A few vendors were still there, packing up, but we didn't see Sunset Acres farm among them. We were really hoping for more goat cheese! 

After we got back to the room I even went back down to A&B Naturals to see if by some chance Sunset Acres had dropped off some cheese while they were in town for the market. No, said the woman at the register, but it's supposed to be coming soon. It's her favorite cheese too and she misses it!

I ended up going to Hannaford and getting a small package of Seal Cove plain goat cheese just to tide us over. It's not as good as Sunset Acres, but still very tasty on our buckwheat and sesame rice crackers.

As usual, the rest of the afternoon was spent reading and researching the area we explored today! And workig up an appetite for supper.

I submitted my order to Limon Grille just after 4:00pm, and we drove down to pick it up in the misty drizzle. The restaurant wasn't busy at the time, and our order was nearly ready by the time we arrived. In less than 5 minutes, we were headed home already with a huge bag of goodies! 

We had forgotten how large the meals are at Limon Grille (hint for next time: order just one sandwich to split - it's more than enough!) But we wanted to try different things, so we ended up with the grilled wings, which came with a cabbage slaw and two sauces: their standard green sauce and something that tasted like a mayo-honey-mustard mix (this one was excellent on the wings!). We also got a chicken doner and a grilled fish sandwich, both of which we'd had last year and really enjoyed. And we also got an order of the phyllo cigars, which were another repeat from last year that are always really good. Everything was so well prepared and flavorful. I remembred how great the fish sandwich was last year, but I didn't quite recall how good the chicken sandwich was. It has a very different flavor than the fish sandwich and includes pickled onions. Whatever spice they put on the wings is really good, too. Savory and slight spicy. Even though it tasted nothing like them, I was reminded of my mother's grilled wings with plum sauce. There is something about grilling wings that's even better than deep frying them.

This evening, full from our awesome Mediterranean meal, while we were looking at the map of today's tracklog, Rich recalled a place that he used to ride his bike to. We're pretty sure he even took me there at least once. It's at the northeastern corner of the northern Breakneck Pond. And in looking at the maps, it seemed clear that the little road/path he took to reach this nice boulder that he used to rest on was actually the southestern terminus of one of the old roads we've been trying to locate. And not only that, but the old Fawn Pond path branched off to the north somewhere around this point, too. Rich offered to take me back there one of these days so we can check it out. So cool! It's so exciting when an old memory surfaces like this and loops right back around to something we've been researching or investigating in the field.

We've determined that the best way to access the spot should be via the carriage road off Eagle Lake that heads toward Half Moon Pond. This should be the shortest as well as the easiest way to get to the spot, and from there we can see what else we might be able to locate. I already have coordinates in my GPS for a set of steps, supposedly from the Fawn Pond path, that should be about a quarter mile north of the spot we're aiming for. The only question is whether the steps or any part of that area is still accessible.
