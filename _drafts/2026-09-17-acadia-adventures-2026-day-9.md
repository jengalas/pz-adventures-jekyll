At breakfast (the usual oatmeal and banana, and a shared blueberry muffin) we discussed what we wanted to do this morning. The sky was cloudy, which I know Rich was looking forward to in order to do some forest photography. And I still wanted to go for a run around Witch Hole Pond, and attempt to look for the quarry I couldn't find last year. So that became our plan.

We thought we'd check out the situation near the DUck Brook Bridge first. If it was too busy, we could instead park at the Paradise Hill overlook and walk down to the shortcut trail, using that as our entrance point to the carriage roads. But there were only three other cars on Duck Brook Road near the bridge - I've never seen it that deserted. We parked there and walked over the bridge together. Then I began my run, first by taking the old Fern Path instead of the carriage road going down the hill. The Fern Path is an excellent alternative to the long slog of the carriage road hill in this section, particularly if you like trail running. It's narrow and just a bit rocky and rooted, and there are some decent elevation changes. 

I emerged on the carriage road near the eastern reaches of Witch Hole Pond and then continued on, stopping at a few points to see if I could locate any pitcher plants. I didn't see any, even though I know we've found them here in the past. I did see a beaver in Witch Hole Pond. 

I was really enjoying running the rolling hills of the carriage roads, and the coordinates for the quarry came up almost too soon. I must have benen significantly off last year, because this time it only took about 3 minutes of wandering through the brush to spot the large opening in the trees, and then to work my way over to the HUGE quarry. I don't know how you could miss it! It was very interesting and looked a lot like Dorr's quarry that we visited a few years ago.

I was feeling like I wanted more than the 1 kilometer that would remain in my run after the quarry if I continued in the counterclockwise direction, so instead I decided to double back and retrace my steps instead. I had never run the loop in the clockwise direction, so it was all basically new. STill enjoyed the rolling hills and the sights along the way, including a turtle nest that apparently all the baby turtles had escaped from, since I saw a group of discarded egg shells.

I took the Fern Path back toward the bridge, and happened to run into Rich on the way! He had decided to do a photo walk along the trail, and he was susprirsed to see me coming in this direction. I'm doubly glad now thatI went that way because otherwise it might have taken a little while to find him.

I finished out my 9km+ run while he took a slow walk back to the bridge. We walked down toward the brook, whichis low this year, and took a few photos. 

After the run, I was feeling in need of protein (that is my excuse, anyway) so I suggested getting a lobster crepe at Sunrise Cafe. Rich dropped me off and waited somewhere on the pier while I waited in the very long line at Sunrise.

I must have really lucked out and gotten the last of the prepared lobster salad, because they person behind me also tried to order a lobster crepe and she was told that it would take at least 25 minutes. I was so glad that after waiting about that long in the line, my crepe only took about 5 minutes to be prepared. And somehow, just somehow, a slice of blueberry pie also made its way into my bag!

We ate the lobster crepes on the porch, as usual, and it was a really delicious lunch.  Perfectly filling but we didn't end up overly stuffed, because later in the afternoon ...

It's time for our every-other-day pizza at Rosalie's! Today's choice was a medium with sausage. We were the first ones at the door, as usual,  but today it didn't take long for a line to form. The couple behind us sat in the booth next to us (we got the first booth on the first floor, as we have each time so far this trip). The man and Rich were ordering at the same time. Rich said that he ordered a medium with sausage. Then the guy, who was at the register next to him, also ordered a medium with sausage. The cashier asked for Rich's name, and he said "Rich." The other cashier asked for the other guy's name, and he said "Rich." Seemed so bizarre! Rich told me the story when he sat down. And when the waiter brought over our pizza, he commented jokingly on how weird it was that they were both Richs that had ordered a medium sausage pizza. It may have caused some momentary confusion in the pizza kitchen!

In addition to the pizza, which was amazing as always, we had two large Staycation Lagers again. Somehow we ate the whole pizza, even though we were feeling stuffed toward the end.

As the other Rich was leaving, I said to Rich, hey, you should ask him if he's the other Rich with the medium sausage. (Um, wait, that didn't come out right!)

We decided to take a walk past MDI Ice Cream just to see what flavors they had, not that we had room for any dessert this evening. They really didn't have very much, disappointingly. The blueberry ice cream looked good, but I didn't see much of interest in the few sorbets they had (strawberry and maybe lemon-mint). 

I suggested taking the cemetery route back to the room. As we got closer to the cemetery, we noticed a sign that said "Hidden Barn Books." Hmm ... what could this be?! Well, it's a new independent bookstore in an historic barn from the 1880s. Such a warm and inviting space! I'm sure we could have spent hours there exploring their offerings and relaxing in the upstairs loft space, which has plenty of tables and chairs and booths for reading, meetings, board game playing and puzzle solving. They even have a puzzle swap, and the really cool part is that there was an Edward Gorey puzzle there for the taking! It's just too bad I didn't have anything to swap. After browsing all of the shelves (and finding quite a few books we'd like to buy, but won't be able to bring everything home with us) we ended up buying a small journal with sloths on the cover. :grin: 

We took a slow walk back, and enjoyed a little bit of wine and a few videos, in addition to looking at restaurant menus trying to figure out where we'd like to eat dinner tomorrow.
