Expecting a large and filling dinner at Havana this evening, we kept our breakfast very simple and light this morning: just some oatmeal and half of a strawberry yogurt for each of us. We were expecting rain today too, so we planned on having a very easy, relaxing day. 

Our only real idea was to take a drive over to the bae of Kebo Mountain, where we wanted to investigate the status of the entrance from the Park Loop Road onto the Gorge Path, which eventually leads up the gorge between Cadillac and Dorr Mountains, and then eventually southwest to the summit of Cadillac.

My notes on our "quandary" as taken over the winter were as follows:

> When we hiked in this area in 2010, we took the path (orange in the image here) east of Kebo Brook. But the current NPS GIS data (as well as other online sources like Alltrails) show the trail *west* of Kebo Brook to be the current route. 
>
> ![Excerpt from Pathmakers](https://images.planetzhanna.com/2026/09/13/gorge-entrance.jpg){:.float-right} In this image from _Pathmakers_, as of the time of its publication the route west of the brook was abandoned (its purple color indicates that, as well as the description in the book for Path #320). So it seems that at some point this section of the Gorge Path was rerouted east of the brook, and then at some point rerouted back onto the original alignment! Very interesting. We will have to check this out in person. 
> 
> It is also on our list because (see below) on the MapAdventures maps of Acadia the two sides of the map have conflicting information. It isn’t clear from the street view what is currently correct, although it does appear that the official parking area is over the bridge to the east side of the brook, but a set of steps goes down to the west, crossing over the brook (very difficult to tell, though).
>
> - AMC map, 1988 - trail west of Kebo Brook
- AMC map, 1993 - trail west of Kebo Brook (crossing over from parking area on the east side)
- AMC map, 2000 - trail east of Kebo Brook
- AMC map, 2005 - trail east of Kebo Brook
- Discovery map, 2010 - trail east of Kebo Brook
- MapAdventures map, 2015 (3rd edition) - trail on wider view shown on eastern side of brook, while on the detailed view it is shown on the western side.
- AMC map, 2017 - trail west of Kebo Brook
- MapAdventures map, 2022 (4th edition) - trail on wider view shown on eastern side of brook, while on the detailed view it is shown on the western side.
- MapAdventures map, 2025 (5th edition) - trail on wider view shown on eastern side of brook, while on the detailed view it is shown on the western side.

So while it appears that the original alignment had the trail on the western side of the brook, it was later switched to an existing (possibly abandoned) old road or trail on the eastern side of the brook for a time in the early 2000s, and then switched back to following the brook on its western side. We wanted to confirm this, and also to see if we could locate whatever remains of the old road or trail on the eastern side of the brook.

But first, while we were on our way to the Park Loop Road, we happened to drive by the YMCA and noticed that the vendors were just setting up for the weekly farmers market. It had completely slipped my mind that the market would be held this morning (Sunday, as always). We were a few minutes early, but everyone was happy to accommodate us even if they were still setting up their stands. We sampled some delicious smoked salmon and browsed all kinds of maple goodies and smoked meats. Eventually we came across the stand from Pen & Cob, and their jams looked amazing. We bought a jar of wild blueberry and spiced blueberry for Elaine, and a jar of spruce salt for me. I love anything piney or sprucey, and I can hardly wait to try it on eggs, and who knows what else. I was trying to avoid the bakery case, but we ended up somehow getting two biscuits, one a cheddar pimento and one just plain.

Right behind their stand was the one from Sunset Acres Farm. We had to get some goat cheese! I figured it would go well with the biscuits. I've tried most of their flavors before and loved them all, but they had a pesto flavor that looks new (and the woman at the booth confirmed that it's their newest flavor). We bought a tub and, before moving on, Rich asked if they still sold their chickens to Red Sky. "No, not anymore," she said. No elaboration so we have no idea what the deal is there. At least they still have roasted organic half chicken on the menu. 

We began our drive into the park, but with the rain looming, we realized that since we'd entered near Sieur de Monts, we'd have to drive all the way around on the loop road to get to the spot we wanted. We decided to cut through and go back to Bar Harbor instead, and then head up to the entrance on Eagle Lake Road to more directly approach the Gorge Path parking area. 

There were two cars already parked near the entrance to the gorge path, one on either side of the bridge over the brook. It was immediately apparent that although the official parking pulloff is east of the bridge, the current official trail does in fact descend typical Acadian stone steps immediately alongside the bridge and down to the brook, and then ascends gently again on the western side of the brook. I followed it for a short distance to see if I could find the spot where the older trail/road would have joined it from the east, but I didn't spot anything. (I also didn't want to go too far because Rich was waiting for me.)

When I returned to the parking area, I wanted to see if I could locate the old trail/road that had followed the eastern side of the brook. Nothing was evident from the parking area, but I crashed through the woods for a couple minutes, and then I came across it. It was clearly the old trail (wide and level enough that I do think it was a road at one time). Just like the other one, I followed it south for a short distance so I could create enough of a tracklog to see where it was actually going. I didn't want to continue too far because again, Rich was waiting for me, but this time he didn't even know I had found the trail, and I was well out of earshot of him by this point.

I returned to the parking area, and while it seemed that the old road may have continued northeast for a short stretch, leading straight out to the road, I was close enough to the parking area that I just went through the woods and straight to the car.

We continued a short distance along the Park Loop Road to another pulloff, where Rich wanted to look around to see if there was a trailhead sign (just wondering why the pulloff was there). We didn't see any signs, but I looked to my right and saw a clear path heading into the woods! I had to follow it to see where it went.

It continued on, and eventually reached the Stratheden Path. And just about 150 feet beyond that path was one of the cairns that we had found years ago, that marked the path to the Kebo steps! So cool to have made this connection. There was a small piece of orange flagging tape tied around a tree near the cairn, so someone else must be marking it for their own use as well. I don't know if the trail we found was ever a historical trail or just a modern shortcut trail that locals use, but either way, we'll put it on our map! Could it have been part of the Kebo Mountain east side trail (#374 in Pathmakers)?

As soon as I returned to the car and we began driving the Park Loop Road again, it began to sprinkle. I was so glad to at least have finished up the adventure we had in mind for today. We drove through some light showers as we made our way back to town.

Back at the room, we bundled up (it was damp and chilly by this point, and getting breezy) and sat on the porch for a while. We had coffee and tea and shared the cheddar pimento biscuit, slightly warmed and spread with the pesto goat cheese. Such a great snack! We didn't want to have too much else to eat at this point, but we eventually gave in to the temptation to have the other biscuit as well. Unfortunately, something must have gone wrong because although that biscuit was perfectly browned just like the other one, it wasn't fully baked through on the inside. Oh well.

We spent the rest of the afternoon reading and exploring the maps, and then made our way down to Havana in the now-moderate rain for our 4:00pm reservation. They let us in early, probably because of the rain, but we had to wait a few minutes while they finished up their staff meeting. Then we were taken to our table, which was fortunately the nice little table by the front window that we always try to get. (I had requested it when I made the reservation, but you never know.)  Everything was looking good until a family with a tiny baby and another little girl wandered in. You've got to be kidding me. There was plenty of shrieking in the early part of our meal, but thankfully they ate pretty quickly and left.

Our server was Harley, who I'm pretty sure we've had before. Very friendly and good at his job. He brought out the amuse bouche, which tonight was honeydew rounds topped with creamy goat feta and pumpkin seeds. As always we had mojitos, and the mushroom spring roll appetizer. We also decided to try something new: the tuna appetizer, which was spectacular. The citrusy sauce was so perfect, I would have just drunk it right off the plate if I could have done so without making a mess. We had some of the Italian bread left from the bread basket, so we used that to soak up as much of the sauce as we could.

Then our entrees came out, also spectacular as usual. We had the halibut, perfectly cooked and in a light curry sauce. The scallops and shrimp were served with a saffron polenta that was unusual in a very good way. It was so pleasant and quiet after those kids left, and we were able to enjoy most of our meal in peace.

While we were waiting for dessert to come out, we noticed a goofy (again, in a good way) couple out in the street in the rain next to their camper van. The woman was holding a lobster roll, and she put on a lobster-claw headband for the man to take a photo of her while she was chowing down on the lobster roll. It was hilarious and Rich gave them a thumbs up, which they returned. Too funny. They got in the van and she continued eaitng the roll, then they drove off. 

Our dessert was so good, I wish I had room for more! It was a creamy guava cheesecake parfait with guava puree on the bottom, and a crispy oat streusel and some chopped fruit on top. We think there were gooseberries and maybe even rhubarb in there. Just amazing! Rich also ordered a Havana Martini (with pineapple juice) for dessert, and that was really good as well. It's the kind of drink that you can't feel until you stand up and try to walk back home. Fortunately we had no trouble there, and the rain had mostly stopped by the time we were heading back.
