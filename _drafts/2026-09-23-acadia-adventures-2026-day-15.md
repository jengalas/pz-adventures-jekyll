I woke up with the usual last-day ambivalence - we have only one day left ... but we have a whole day! It's always fun to do something a little special or unusual on the last day of our trip. I wasn't sure what to suggest for today;I wanted to leave the ultimate decision up to Rich and whatever he felt like, but my three ideas were for me to run the Aunt Betty Pond carriage road loop (while Rich either walked part of it, or found a sunny spot to sit and read); to return to the Lake Wood area and hike in from the northern end of Breakneck Road, trying to complete the road to Lake Wood that Rich remembers from all those years ago; or to hike Eliot Mountain to look for the geocache again and see if the original logbook is still in place, and maybe even check on the SAVAGE MTN triangulation station.

It took some time over oatmeal to decide, but we ended up choosing the Breakneck Road option. Rich was feeling good and was interested in going for a walk, so as long as he was up for it, I was very interested in exploring with him rather than on my own.

We parked in the small lot at the northern end of Breakneck Road, hoping as always that we would have the place to ourselves. While we were getting ready to begin our hike, and older man with a very young girl and dog came down the road. They were pleasant and I'm sure had only been out for a brief morning walk. They were the only people we saw for quite some time. 

It was a very chilly but sunny morning, and we were warm enough as we wandered the road, looking for mushrooms, snakes, and anything else we might find along the way. I showed Rich the spring surrounded by rocks, which I'm not sure that he has seen before.

> A natural spring along Breakneck Road was excavated and lined with stones to provide fresh water for walkers.

Climbing the hill toward Lake Wood, once we reached it, helped to warm us up quickly, even though we were still strolling slowly, carefully stepping over deadfall and trying not to trample any mushrooms. It didn't take long before we reached the point which I'd never passed before, and which should take us to Lake Wood. 

Immediately,Rich was saying that it didn't quite look familiar, especially because once we passed the endpoint of my former travels, the road began to descend rather sharply, and soon became a very rocky, mildly steep, washed-out trail. Rich said that he never would have ridden down anythign like that, and what he recalled was an easy road that took him right to the shore of the lake. He was thinking that it was the southwestern "corner" of Lake Wood. The trail was so rugged at this point that I set off for the last few hundred feet on my own, making it to the lake shore in a dense conifer forest that looked nothing like what he had described. Strange! 

I took a few photos and came back up the hill. Rich still kept saying that he didnt remember coming down any hill like this at all. So I began to wonder if some other road had branched off at the point where the main road took a nearly 90 degree turn.

We returned to that point and poked around in the woods for some time, but couldn't spot anything much that looked like a road or trail other than at a stream crossing. The signs of use there may simply have been deer coming to the stream to drink.

Without anything ekse to go on today, we began to head back. When we reached the road to Fawn Pond, which at least now is much more obvious than the road we took to Lake WOod, we both began to wonder if that wasn't actually the way he had gone instead. Coudl he have mistaken Fawn Pond for Lake Wood? It doesn't seem that likely based on the size of the two bodies of water and how they look, but the road does seem to match up a little better for Fawn Pond. I even recall when I was here the other day I took a wrong turn, following the obvious road instead of the footpath, and ended up at the wrong side of Fawn Pond. (I realized it quickly and got back to the path, but it did make me wonder, again, if that obvious road was the path he had taken.) Next time we're here, we will try this same walk again, but this time veer onto the road to Fawn Pond, and see if that rings any bells for Rich.

Walking down the road toward Breakneck, just past a small erosional area (maybe part of the rockfall from several years ago?) I spotted a man sitting on the road with a mountain bike next to him. Turns out he had been a mechanic a tthe old Bar Harbor bike shop for years. We got the whole story on how eveyrthig changed when Joe and Al retired and the shop was bought by some banker from Ohio who just wanted to cash in on the e-bike thing.

He was defintiely an old-school mountain biker dude straight out of the 90s, quite refreshing to run into these days. And a basic Mainer, very cool. He was eating what looked and smelled like a basil-tomato-mozzarella sandiwch on a bagel. It was fun to come across him on the trail. He said he had just found the trail abuot a month ago and didn't know if anyone else even knew about it. Rich told him that the reason we were here today was that he'd found it decades ago!

Continuing on, we turned back onto Breakneck Road and here I began to feel chilly for some reason. We still walked slowly, enjoying the sun through the pines, and the mushrooms popping up here and there. At one moment I spotted a slithery flash in the grass to the side of the road. Then again. A snake!!! It was a cute littel garter snake. After that first skittish encounter, he didn't move a muscle and we were able to get a good look at him. So fitting that we would see one more snake on our last day. 

And that was the end of our lovely hike together. Two more mountain bikers passed us as we were very close to the end of the road, but otherwise, we saw no one and loved having the place to ourselves.

Our standard "last supper" is always pizza at Rosalie's. It's the best, most bittersweet way to end each wonderful span of time we spend here. Planning as usual to arrive early enough to be the first ones in the door, we were a bit surprised to see that, for the first time during this trip, we were not the first to arrive. There was an older woman already waiting near the door. We began chatting with her, and learned that she and her husband live in Mariaville, a small town north of ELlsworth (nearly an hour's drive away) and really only come to Bar Harbor for Rosalie's pizza. (By the way, Mariaville is pronounced Mariah-ville, as in Mariah Carey, not Maria-ville as in Maria Callas.) Her husband showed up a few minutes later; I assume he had been parking the car somewhere nearby. They were both very kind and sweet, and kind of reminded us of old-school hippies. Soon another couple joined us, who were originally from Connecticut but are now Bar Harbor residents. I thought this meant that the line would start to grow quickly, but we were the only three couples waiting when the door opened at 4:00pm.

The couple from Mariaville sat at "our" first booth, and when I joking mentioned "oh, they got our booth" the woman offered several times to switch. I certainly didn't want to do that because they were there first, and I was more than happy for them to have their first choice. We sat in the next booth and it was nice to have someone we sort of "knew" sitting near us. Rich told them about the tokens, which they weren't aware of. And they ordered an extra-large pizza with mushrooms and onions, and beers, like we did.

We had been talking all day throughout our hike about what type of pizza we'd like to get. We chose a small house veggie to eat there, and we got three small pizzas to go, to bring home (plain for my father, and a mushroom and a house veggie for us). We were still a bit hungry, so we also ordered a single slice of pepperoni (sliced in half lengthwise) to share just to top things off. I had an Allagash White Ale and Rich had a Baxter Staycation Lager.

It was too cold for ice cream, and we had the three takeout pizzas to carry back to the room anyway, so we skipped dessert today. Later on, while watching Sidetrack Adventures, we ate a few Puffys and sipped some wine, as we usually do while watching those videos. It was such a peaceful way to end a soul-heartening trip together.
